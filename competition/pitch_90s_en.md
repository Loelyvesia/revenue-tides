# 90-Second Pitch

When people see a game-revenue chart, they often ask which title is larger or which update caused growth. Public disclosures do not support those shortcuts. A reported number may cover one title or an entire IP, mobile only or several platforms, and the reporting definition may change within the same company.

Revenue Tides builds nine fiscal-quarter series and 75 observations from official disclosures by four listed game companies. It compares change only within stable definitions. Direct observations remain separate from values derived from official totals and rounded shares. Missing data is never zero, and an event in the same quarter is context rather than proof of causality.

Three headline Findings show why this matters. Lineage M's legacy and royalty-integrated definitions differ by KRW 6.797 to 13.455 billion in the same overlapping quarters; that is a definition effect, not growth. Stellar Blade rises from KRW 7.012 billion to 65.719 billion in its PC-launch quarter, a gain of 837.3%, but no PC-only amount is disclosed. Black Desert falls 20.7%, recovers to a new peak, then ends 30.8% below that peak—an important sequence that a simple “decline” label would erase.

Quarter Prism lets a reviewer move through quarters while seeing value, scope, metric version, evidence, uncertainty, events, and source together. The result is not a global revenue leaderboard. It is a reproducible way to see what each commercial number means and where interpretation must stop.
