# Abstract

Revenue Tides investigates whether official public company disclosures can support a reproducible study of game commercial trajectories. The project uses the fiscal quarter as its primary temporal unit and builds a curated corpus of nine publisher-defined game or IP series from four listed companies. The corpus contains 75 observations from 2024Q3 to 2026Q2, 26 curated official events, and three issuer-attributed claims.

Each observation retains entity scope, reporting definition, metric version, evidence class, missingness, period boundaries, and source provenance. Directly reported values are kept separate from values derived from official company totals and rounded title shares. All 18 derived observations preserve compatible lower and upper bounds and whether quarter-to-quarter direction remains stable across those bounds. Six frozen Findings address same-quarter reporting-definition differences, a peak in Stellar Blade's PC-launch quarter, bounded rebound and recovery states, persistent decline within Lineage W's separate royalty-integrated window, and directional uncertainty caused by rounded shares.

The project's signature interaction, Quarter Prism, turns a selected quarter into an analytical reading unit that combines value, scope, version, evidence, uncertainty, contemporaneous event context, issuer attribution, and source access. The Tidal Ledger visual system makes calibration, discontinuity, and contextual separation visible. Revenue Tides does not create a cross-company revenue leaderboard, infer causality from event timing, or treat observed-window non-recovery as permanent decline.
